# Purview Ops — Site Restructure

Driven by the prospect list. 26 qualified companies out of 50, and what they have in common changes what the site should lead with.

Three changes. What the site calls its market, what the home page argues, and a new page.

---

## 1. What the data said

**Government is a third of the qualified market.** Only 2 of 26 have it as primary buyer, but 11 have it as secondary. Routeware, Cirba Solutions, Moleaer, Loop Global, InductEV, Recycle Track Systems, Biohabitats and others all sell into municipalities, districts, or agencies alongside something else.

**Half the list would not call itself climate tech.** Routeware is waste and recycling software. Cirba is battery recycling. Moleaer does nanobubbles for water treatment. Biohabitats does ecological restoration. Quanta Technology does grid engineering. None of these lead with climate on their own sites and several would bristle at the label.

**The size clusters tighter than the offer describes.** Median is roughly 105 employees with 19 people selling. Nothing on the qualified list is founder-led.

---

## 2. Change one. What the site calls its market

**Current:** "Go to market engineering and revenue operations for climate technology companies."

**The problem:** a waste software company selling to municipalities reads that and concludes the site is not about them. So does a grid engineering firm and a water treatment company.

**Replace with:** "Go to market engineering and revenue operations for energy and environmental infrastructure."

Why that phrase. It covers utilities, water, waste, grid, renewables, and efficiency without requiring anyone to self-identify as climate. It also matches how these companies describe themselves, which is usually by what they build rather than by what problem it solves.

**Where it changes**

| Location | Current | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Hero subhead | climate technology companies | energy and environmental infrastructure |
| /about opening | for climate technology companies | for companies selling into energy, water, waste, and grid infrastructure |
| /about "Why climate" heading | Why climate | Why this market |
| Meta descriptions | climate technology | energy and environmental infrastructure |

**What does not change.** The /about section explaining why this market is still allowed to say climate, because that section is about motive and the word is accurate there. It is the front door language that has to be broader.

## 3. Change two. The home page leads with public timing

Right now "a lot of what decides your timeline is public" is item three of four in the differentiator section. It should be the argument, not a bullet.

### Why it earns the position

It is the only claim on the site that no competitor can make. The countable market claim is good but a generalist could learn it. The public timing claim requires knowing that board minutes, bond measures, capital improvement plans, and permit filings exist and what each one means about when somebody buys.

It is also true today and demonstrable from public sources. It does not require a product to exist.

### The differentiator section, reordered and rewritten

Current order is market is countable, customers buy differently, timing is public, first deal is a trial.

New order puts timing first and expands it.

> ### We know how your business actually works
>
> **A lot of what decides your timeline is already published.** Your buyers are utilities, municipalities, districts, and agencies. When they decide to spend money, they do it in a public meeting, with minutes. The capital plan is on a website. The bond measure was on a ballot. The permit is in a county database. Most of it exists months before anyone posts an RFP, and by the time the RFP is out the specification was usually written with somebody already in the room.
>
> **Your market is small enough to count.** There are only so many companies and institutions that fit what you sell, which means you can know the real number instead of estimating it, and you can know who is in it.
>
> **Your customers do not all buy the same way.** A utility, a municipality, and a private operator have almost nothing in common except that you sell to all three. Blending them into one pipeline number gives you an average that describes none of them.
>
> **A first deal is a trial.** It either works or you do not get the second one, which makes what happens after the close matter as much as the close.

Same four items. First one is longer and does the work.

### Hero panel, second option

The current hero panel shows 64d against 171d, meaning the survivorship correction. It is good and it stays for now.

**Worth considering later:** a second panel or an alternate showing a timeline. Bond passes, capital plan published, consultant engaged, RFP posts, award. With a marker showing where most vendors start and where the window actually opened.

That is the flagship argument rendered as a picture and it would be the strongest visual on the site. It needs the validation work to have run first, so it is noted rather than specified.

## 4. Change three. A new page

**Route:** `/timing`

**Nav label:** Timing

Sits between Work and Method.

### What it is

The page explaining how institutional and utility buying actually gets decided and where the public record shows it, plus what it costs to respond once an RFP posts and to know who is deciding.

**Not a monitoring product page.** The signal half is in the same register as `/method`. It demonstrates that you understand how these buyers work, which is the credibility claim. The response and the committee are a different thing, since both describe work performed. If the signal engine is ever built, this page gains a product section rather than needing a rewrite.

### Structure and copy

In `purview_timing_page_copy.md`, which is the record of what ships. That
file carries the opening, the six sections, the close and the build notes,
including the one about the close not claiming an audit check.

This document is the plan that produced the page. It stops at the decision
to build it, so a copy change does not have to be made in two places.

---

## 5. What this does not change

**The four stages stay, including Seed.** Nothing on the qualified list is founder-led, but that is a targeting decision, not an offer decision. Seed clients have the longest lifetime value and are the hardest to close cold. Keep the offer, stop targeting it in outreach until there are A and B references.

**The audit stays the front door.** Unchanged.

**`/method` stays as written.** `/timing` is adjacent to it, not a replacement.

**No monitoring claims on `/timing`.** Nothing on that page says Purview monitors anything, and the monitoring product is unvalidated and stays off the site until phase one runs. The page does describe work performed, which is the response and the committee, and neither of those is the signal engine.

3. `/timing` as a new page, plus the nav item.
4. Later, once phase one validation has run, the timeline hero panel and a product section on `/timing`.
