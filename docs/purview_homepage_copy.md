## The enemy

Your product is good. Your reps can sell. Something between those two facts is costing you deals and nobody can point at it.

It shows up as leads nobody followed up on. Reps spending mornings researching instead of selling. A number in the dashboard that nobody quite believes. Leads you disqualified last quarter that would qualify today, sitting where nobody will look at them again.

None of that is a people problem. It is what happens when the systems underneath a sales team were built one piece at a time by whoever had a minute.

We find where it is leaking and we fix it.

## The differentiator

### We know how your business actually works

Your market is small enough to count. There are only so many companies that fit what you sell, which means you can know the real number instead of estimating it, and you can know who's in it.

Your customers don't all buy the same way. A utility, a developer, and a corporate sustainability team have almost nothing in common except that you sell to all three. Blending them into one pipeline number gives you an average that describes none of them.

A lot of what decides your timeline is public. Grant cycles, budget approvals, procurement calendars. Most of it is published somewhere before anyone announces anything.

And a first deal is a trial. It either works or you don't get the second one, which makes what happens after the close matter as much as the close.

## Stage selector

### Where are you stuck?

**The founder still closes everything**

You have a handful of customers and most of what happened lives in one person's head. It works until it doesn't, usually somewhere around thirty or forty accounts, and by then there's no history to go back and reconstruct.

**You raised on numbers you can't reproduce**

The funnel math in the deck came from a spreadsheet someone built once. You have a real team now and nobody can rebuild it from the system. Meanwhile a lot of inbound never gets a first touch, because routing was never anyone's job.

**Every number is two things averaged together**

You finally have enough volume to compute win rate and cycle length, which means you finally have enough to compute them wrong. A utility deal and a corporate deal don't behave alike, and blending them gives you a number that describes neither.

**Growth depends on logos you have to keep buying**

Your base is big enough that percentage growth slows on its own. Repeat business and expansion should be carrying more of it by now, and nothing in the system separates that out so you can see how much it actually carries.

## The audit

### The RevOps Audit

Two weeks. $1,000, credited in full against your first month if you keep going.

We start by sending you a short intake doc, before we look at anything. We want to hear where you think it is leaking, in your own words, from whoever on your team has an opinion. Then we get read only access and go find out.

Most companies are running three versions of the same revenue engine. The one leadership thinks they built. The one written down in playbooks and CRM fields. And the one your team actually uses to get work done every day, which usually involves a spreadsheet somebody maintains by hand. The gap between those three is where most of this lives.

**What we check.**

- Your actual market size, counted from the real list rather than the number on a slide from two years ago
- Whether your ICP describes the customers you have actually won, or the ones you meant to
- How many of your inbound leads got contacted at all, and how fast
- Records sitting with no owner, or an owner who left
- Your real sales cycle, counting the deals that stalled instead of only the ones that closed
- Whether your win rate holds up when you split it by segment
- How much of your team's week is spent fixing things that should have worked the first time

**What you get.** A findings document with a number attached to each leak, ordered by what to do first. It is yours either way. If you never talk to us again, you can hand it to whoever fixes it.
